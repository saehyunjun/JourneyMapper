WCT_GLP-1 Interviews_Participant 11_Transcript
· Asian American, male

Interviewer: First of all, thank you for agreeing to take part in this interview today. As I mentioned earlier, this research is intended to better understand the experiences of people using GLP-1 medications for weight management and to explore awareness of and willingness to participate in clinical trials. Before we begin, do you have any questions for me?

Participant: No, I think I’m okay. I read through the information beforehand.

Interviewer: Great. So I’m going to start by telling you a little bit about the type of clinical trial we’re discussing. This study is focused on weight maintenance for people who have previously lost weight on a GLP-1 medication and then discontinued semaglutide for at least 28 days while maintaining a stable weight for around three months. While answering, we’d like you to imagine yourself in that maintenance phase. Does that make sense?

Participant: Yeah, definitely.

Interviewer: Okay. So my first question is: what factors might make you consider stopping taking a GLP-1 medication?

Participant: Honestly, the biggest one would be access. I’m fortunate right now because my employer insurance covers most of the medication cost, but I’m very aware that could change at any point. If I suddenly had to pay out of pocket, I don’t know that I could justify over a thousand dollars a month long term. The other thing would probably be if I developed some kind of serious side effect or complication. But overall the medication has improved my quality of life enough that I really don’t want to stop.

Interviewer: Can you expand a little on what you mean by quality of life improvements?

Participant: Sure. Obviously I lost weight, but I also sleep better, my blood pressure improved, and honestly my anxiety around food is much lower now. I didn’t realize how much mental energy I spent thinking about food all day until it stopped. It’s quieter in my head now.

Interviewer: And if you did have to stop, would you consider trying a different medication?

Participant: Yeah, absolutely. Especially if another option was cheaper or easier to access. I also follow a lot of the newer medications being developed, so if something came out with fewer side effects or maybe less frequent injections, I’d definitely be interested.

Interviewer: Would you consider participating in a clinical trial for a medication that hasn’t yet been approved?

Participant: I think so, yes.

Interviewer: What factors would influence that decision for you?

Participant: Transparency would matter a lot. I’d want clear communication about risks, side effects, what happens if something goes wrong, things like that. Access is another factor. If participation meant I could continue receiving treatment, that would honestly be pretty motivating. I also generally support medical research. I work in tech, so I’m probably more comfortable than average with experimental systems and iteration.

Interviewer: Okay. How would you feel about participating in a placebo-controlled study where there’s a chance you may not receive active medication?

Participant: That’s probably the hardest part for me. The idea of regaining the weight or losing the benefits I’ve experienced is honestly scary. Intellectually I understand why placebo groups are necessary, but emotionally it’s harder. I think the length of the placebo period would really matter.

Interviewer: For this study, the treatment period would be around 36 weeks with monthly injections. How would you feel about that?

Participant: Monthly injections sound manageable. Honestly better than weekly. The bigger issue would probably be logistics around appointments.

Interviewer: What sort of logistical concerns?

Participant: Mainly scheduling and travel. I travel pretty frequently for work, and if appointments had to happen in person at the same clinic every month, that could become difficult pretty quickly.

Interviewer: Would home visits or telehealth options make participation easier?

Participant: Definitely. If some of the follow-up visits could happen remotely, or if basic things like blood draws could happen locally, that would make the study feel much more realistic for me.

Interviewer: How likely would you be to use compounded GLP-1 medications versus approved products?

Participant: I’m open to compounded medications, but cautiously. I’d want to know where it’s coming from and that the pharmacy is reputable. Cost-wise I understand why people go that route though.

Interviewer: What kinds of things might motivate you to participate in a clinical trial? For example financial compensation, meal vouchers, or weight-loss support after the study?

Participant: Financial compensation is nice, but honestly convenience would probably matter more for me. Things like flexible scheduling, remote check-ins, maybe nutrition counseling or fitness tracking support would feel more useful than meal replacement products.

Interviewer: What about travel support or transportation services?

Participant: Transportation itself wouldn’t really help me because I have a car and travel frequently anyway. It’s more the time commitment. If I have to lose half a workday every visit, that becomes difficult.

Interviewer: Would educational materials or webinars be useful during a trial?

Participant: Yeah, definitely. Especially evidence-based nutrition guidance. There’s so much misinformation online around GLP-1s that having trustworthy resources from the study team would be valuable.

Interviewer: Have you considered clinical trials before?

Participant: I’ve looked into them a few times actually. Mostly online. But a lot of the studies I found were either too far away or required more in-person visits than I could realistically commit to.

Interviewer: What other approaches have you tried for weight loss before using GLP-1s?

Participant: Pretty much everything. Calorie tracking, intermittent fasting, keto, personal trainers, intense exercise programs. Some worked temporarily, but I always regained the weight eventually.

Interviewer: Are you aware of oral GLP-1 medications currently being developed?

Participant: Yeah, I’ve been following those pretty closely.

Interviewer: What are your thoughts on them?

Participant: I think oral options will make these medications accessible to a much wider group of people. A lot of people are uncomfortable with injections. Personally though, I’d probably still prefer injections because I like the consistency. I know exactly when I took it, and it feels more reliable to me.

Interviewer: So if you had to choose between a newer injectable medication and an oral medication, which would you lean toward?

Participant: Probably the injectable, assuming the efficacy was stronger. If the oral version eventually became equally effective though, I’d definitely reconsider.

Interviewer: Okay. That’s all of the formal questions I have for you today. Is there anything we haven’t discussed that you think is important for researchers to understand when designing clinical trials?

Participant: I think people underestimate how emotional weight management is. A lot of these conversations focus on numbers or pounds lost, but for many people this medication changes how they think, sleep, socialize, and move through daily life. So if a study design feels disruptive or stressful, that’s going to matter more than researchers sometimes realize.
