WCT_GLP-1 Interviews_Participant 12_Transcript
· Black, female

Interviewer: Thank you again for taking part in the interview today. As we discussed previously, this research is intended to better understand patient experiences with GLP-1 medications for weight management and also to understand perceptions of clinical trials and future treatments. Before we start, do you have any questions for me?

Participant: No, I think I’m okay. I’m interested to hear more about the study though.

Interviewer: Absolutely. So I’m going to start by explaining the type of trial we’re discussing. This is a study focused on weight maintenance for people who have previously lost weight while taking a GLP-1 medication and who have discontinued semaglutide for at least 28 days while maintaining a stable weight for approximately three months. While answering these questions, we’d like you to imagine yourself in that maintenance phase. Does that sound alright?

Participant: Yeah, that makes sense.

Interviewer: Great. So my first question is: what factors might make you consider stopping taking a GLP-1 medication?

Participant: Honestly, cost is the biggest thing for me. Right now I’m covered through insurance, but I’m constantly worried that could change. Every time I hear about policy changes or shortages online, I get nervous because I know how hard it was before starting this medication. I also think if I started having serious side effects that affected my daily life, then I’d probably reconsider. But outside of that, I really don’t want to stop.

Interviewer: Can you tell me a little bit about the changes you’ve experienced since starting treatment?

Participant: Sure. The obvious one is weight loss, but honestly the mental changes have been just as important. I used to think about food constantly. I didn’t realize how exhausting that was until it stopped. My inflammation is lower, my knees hurt less, and I feel more comfortable socially now too. I’m not avoiding pictures anymore, which sounds small but it’s a huge emotional shift.

Interviewer: That makes a lot of sense. And would you consider trying a different medication in the future?

Participant: Yeah, definitely. I think these medications are evolving quickly and I’m open to whatever works best long term. If something came out that was cheaper or had fewer side effects or maybe required less frequent injections, I’d absolutely consider switching.

Interviewer: Would you consider participating in a clinical trial for a medication that has not yet been approved?

Participant: I think I would, yes.

Interviewer: What factors would influence that decision?

Participant: Trust would matter a lot for me. I’d want to know who’s running the study, what protections are in place, and how transparent they are about risks. I’d also want to know what happens after the trial ends because I’d be worried about losing access to a medication that was helping me.

Interviewer: How would you feel about participating in a placebo-controlled study where there’s a chance you wouldn’t receive active medication?

Participant: That part would make me nervous. I understand scientifically why placebo groups are important, but emotionally it’s difficult because I know how quickly I used to regain weight before. It’s not just the weight either. It’s the mental stress and shame that came with it.

Interviewer: The study we’re discussing would involve monthly injections over approximately 36 weeks. How would you feel about that?

Participant: Monthly injections sound okay. Honestly I’d prefer that over weekly. The bigger issue for me would probably be getting to appointments consistently because my work schedule changes a lot.

Interviewer: Would remote visits or home nurse visits help with that?

Participant: Yeah, definitely. Telehealth would make a huge difference. Even if only some visits could happen virtually, that would make participation feel much more realistic for me.

Interviewer: How likely would you be to consider compounded GLP-1 medications compared to approved branded medications?

Participant: I’d consider it, but cautiously. I’d want to make sure it came from a trustworthy pharmacy because there’s a lot of scary information online about counterfeit medications. But I understand why people do it because the branded versions are so expensive.

Interviewer: What kinds of things would motivate you to participate in a clinical trial? For example financial compensation, meal support, or educational resources?

Participant: Financial compensation would help because participating takes time away from work. But honestly, education and support would matter more to me. I’d want nutrition guidance, maybe support groups, or access to professionals who actually understand obesity beyond just telling people to eat less.

Interviewer: Would transportation assistance help?

Participant: A little, but honestly time is the bigger issue. If I have to spend four hours traveling and waiting at a clinic, that’s harder than the actual driving itself.

Interviewer: What types of educational materials would you find valuable during a study?

Participant: I’d like practical information. Meal planning, grocery shopping guidance, realistic exercise ideas, things like that. Also information about what happens after stopping medication because I don’t think people talk enough about maintenance.

Interviewer: Have you considered clinical trials before?

Participant: I’ve looked into them online but never seriously applied. Most of the studies I found were too far away or seemed like too much of a time commitment.

Interviewer: Before starting GLP-1 medications, what other approaches did you try for weight loss?

Participant: Everything. Keto, calorie counting, personal trainers, Weight Watchers, intermittent fasting, bootcamp classes. I could lose some weight temporarily, but I always gained it back eventually. It felt like I was constantly blaming myself.

Interviewer: Are you aware of oral GLP-1 medications currently being developed?

Participant: Yeah, I’ve read a little about them.

Interviewer: What are your thoughts on them?

Participant: I think they’ll help a lot of people who are afraid of injections. Personally though, I kind of like the predictability of injections. I know I took it, and then I don’t have to think about it again for a week. I worry I’d forget a daily pill.

Interviewer: So if you had to choose between a newer injectable medication and an oral medication, which would you lean toward?

Participant: Probably the injectable unless the oral version worked just as well. Convenience matters, but effectiveness matters more to me.

Interviewer: Okay. Those are all the formal questions I have for you today. Is there anything else you think researchers should understand when designing clinical trials for people taking GLP-1 medications?

Participant: I think researchers underestimate how much shame people carry around weight. A lot of participants are going to come into these studies already feeling judged by doctors or by society. So if a study feels difficult, inconvenient, or impersonal, people may quietly drop out instead of asking for help. Feeling supported and respected would make a huge difference.
